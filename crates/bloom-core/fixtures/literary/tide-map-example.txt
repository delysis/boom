At the end of the summer we took down the curtains. Light entered the room with an authority it had never had while we lived there. It touched the bare hooks, the grey patch behind the wardrobe, the bowl in which my father had kept foreign coins. We had spent years calling the room dark. It had only been occupied.

My brother held the ladder while I unfastened the last ring. Dust made a small weather around my hands. Outside, someone was mowing a lawn. The sound came and went as the mower turned, and I waited for each return before moving again, as though the man beyond the hedge were holding something steady for me.

We folded the curtains on the floor. He remembered them as green; I remembered them as blue. Without the window behind them they were neither. A button lay caught in the hem, white and almost weightless. I put it in my pocket. There were boxes for everything else, but no sensible box for a thing that had arrived too late to have a use.
