The council had supplied a form for reporting lost umbrellas, but no box in which to report finding one. Bernard turned it over. The back was reserved for official use.

“It was here when I arrived,” he said.

“That doesn't make it found.”

The woman behind the counter kept her hands folded on a green blotter. Beside her, a fan swung slowly between a dead fern and an open filing cabinet. It blew on neither of them long enough to be useful.

“What would make it found?”

“Someone would have to lose it first.”

Bernard considered claiming that the umbrella was his. It was a good umbrella. Its handle had been repaired with a piece of yellow string, and the repair suggested an owner worth disappointing as little as possible.

“Could I lose it now?”

The woman unfolded her hands. “Not in this office.”

He took the umbrella outside. It had begun to rain. For the first time all morning he was glad of the rules, which had required him to carry it this far.
